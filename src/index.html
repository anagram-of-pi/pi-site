What is π?

Most people could tell you that π equals about 3.14. But there is so much more to it than that!

Of all mathematical constants, π is the most well known, for good reason. It shows up just about everywhere once you start looking for it, and even when you aren't. If you are doing something with circles, π will almost certainly be there. If something is repeating (like sine and cosine), there's a good chance you'll find π.

Typically, π is defined as the ratio between the circumference and diameter of a circle. This value, circumference divided by diameter, is the same for every circle, regardless of size or position. That's what makes π a "constant".

How do we approximate π?

The first ever real attempt at approximating π was done by Archimedes, now called "Archimedes' Polygon Approximation." There are many others now that are able to calculate millions of digits in seconds, however Archimedes gives a fairly intuitive way of calculating it.
